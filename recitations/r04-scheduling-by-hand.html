Measures, per process, are listed below.

  • turnaround = completion − arrival
  • waiting = turnaround − burst
  • response = first run − arrival
Two rules, used in every course problem

1. In SJF and SRTF, equal bursts (or equal remaining times) go to the process that arrived first. 2. In RR, if a new arrival and a process whose quantum just expired enter the ready queue at the same moment, the new arrival is queued first.

FCFS and SJF

2.1FCFS

Chart and waiting times

Waiting 0, 5, 7, 7 gives an average of 4.75. P3 needs 1 unit and waits 7, which is the convoy effect.

2.2SJF (nonpreemptive)

Chart and waiting times

At 0 only P1 has arrived, so it runs to 7 even though shorter jobs arrive meanwhile. At 7 the choice is P2 (4), P3 (1), P4 (4). P3 goes first, then P2 before P4 by rule 1. Waiting 0, 6, 3, 7 gives an average of 4.00.

Part 3

SRTF

Decide again at every arrival, by remaining time. Keep a remaining-time column.

Step by step
  • At t = 0, P1 runs (7 left).
  • At t = 2, P2 arrives with 4, less than P1's 5 left. P1 is preempted and P2 runs.
  • At t = 4, P3 arrives with 1, less than P2's 2 left. P3 runs and finishes at 5.
  • At t = 5, P4 arrives with 4. The remaining times are P1 5, P2 2, P4 4. P2 runs and finishes at 7.
  • At t = 7, P4 (4) goes before P1 (5). P4 finishes at 11, P1 at 16.

Waiting 9, 1, 0, 2 gives an average of 3.00. Response 0, 0, 0, 2 gives an average of 0.50.

Cheat sheet

FCFSArrival order, each to completion. Nonpreemptive.
SJFShortest burst among those that have arrived, to completion. Nonpreemptive.
SRTFShortest remaining time, re-decided at every arrival. Preemptive SJF.
RR, quantum qRun the front of the queue for at most q, then send it to the back.
turnaroundcompletion − arrival
waitingturnaround − burst (time spent ready, not running)
responsefirst run − arrival
