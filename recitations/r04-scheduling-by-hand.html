The measures below are computed per process.

  • turnaround = completion − arrival
  • waiting = turnaround − burst
  • response = first run − arrival
Two rules used in every course problem

1. In SJF and SRTF, equal bursts (or equal remaining times) go to the process that arrived first. 2. In RR, if a new arrival and a process whose quantum just expired enter the ready queue at the same moment, the new arrival is queued first.

2.2SJF (nonpreemptive)

Chart and waiting times

At 0, only P1 has arrived. Therefore, P1 runs to 7, even though shorter jobs arrive in the meantime. At 7, the choice is among P2 (4), P3 (1), and P4 (4). P3 goes first. Then P2 goes before P4 by rule 1. The waiting times are 0, 6, 3, and 7, so the average is 4.00.

Part 3

SRTF

Decide again at every arrival, based on the remaining time. Keep a column of remaining times.

Step by step
  • At t = 0, P1 runs (7 left).
  • At t = 2, P2 arrives with 4, which is less than the 5 that P1 has left. Therefore, P1 is preempted, and P2 runs.
  • At t = 4, P3 arrives with 1, which is less than the 2 that P2 has left. P3 runs and finishes at 5.
  • At t = 5, P4 arrives with 4. The remaining times are 5 for P1, 2 for P2, and 4 for P4. P2 runs and finishes at 7.
  • At t = 7, P4 (4) goes before P1 (5). P4 finishes at 11, and P1 finishes at 16.

The waiting times are 9, 1, 0, and 2, so the average is 3.00. The response times are 0, 0, 0, and 2, so the average is 0.50.

SRTF is best in every column here. However, it needs to know the burst lengths, and a real OS does not know them. In contrast, RR needs no such knowledge and still keeps the response time low. The cost is a higher turnaround.

Cheat sheet

FCFSRuns processes in arrival order, each to completion. It is nonpreemptive.
SJFRuns the arrived process with the shortest burst, to completion. It is nonpreemptive.
SRTFRuns the process with the shortest remaining time, and decides again at every arrival. It is preemptive SJF.
RR, quantum qRuns the front of the queue for at most q, then moves it to the back.
turnaroundcompletion − arrival
waitingturnaround − burst (time spent ready, not running)
responsefirst run − arrival
