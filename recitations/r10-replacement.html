Part 1 · follow along

The string

4 5 3 5 3 2 1 5 3 1 2 1  ·  3 frames, empty at the start

The first five references are the same for every policy: 4, 5 and 3 fill the frames (3 faults), then 5 and 3 hit. Draw a 12-column grid for each policy and try it before opening the answer.

Part 2

FIFO

Trace: 9 faults

Look at reference 11: FIFO evicts 1, the oldest arrival, and 1 is needed at the very next reference.

Part 3

LRU

Trace: 8 faults

Identical to FIFO until reference 11. There, 1 was used at reference 10, 3 at 9 and 5 at 8, so LRU evicts 5; 1 survives and the last reference hits. FIFO and LRU agree whenever arrival order and use order agree. They part ways the moment an old page is used again.

Part 4

OPT

Trace: 6 faults
  • Reference 6 (2): the next uses are 4 never, 5 at 8, 3 at 9, so evict 4.
  • Reference 7 (1): 2 at 11, 5 at 8, 3 at 9, so evict 2.
  • Reference 11 (2): 1 at 12; 5 and 3 never again. Evict 5; evicting 3 is equally optimal.
Part 5

Clock

Trace: 8 faults

Each frame's reference bit is in its corner, and the blue underline is the hand. At reference 6 all three bits are 1. The hand clears them in one full sweep, comes back to frame 0, and replaces 4: with every bit set, clock is FIFO. On this string clock ends up evicting exactly what LRU does. On other strings it can do better or worse; it is an approximation.

Scoreboard

FIFO 9 · LRU 8 · Clock 8 · OPT 6. OPT is the floor. LRU and clock get most of the way there without seeing the future. Check your own strings in the Lecture 16 simulator.

Cheat sheet

FIFOEvict the page loaded earliest.
LRUEvict the page whose last use is oldest.
OPTEvict the page whose next use is farthest away (or never).
ClockAt the hand: bit 1 → clear it and move on; bit 0 → evict. New and referenced pages get bit 1.
open/read/write/lseek/closeThe five file system calls; section 2 of the manual.
lseek(fd, 0, SEEK_CUR)Returns the current offset: "tell".
