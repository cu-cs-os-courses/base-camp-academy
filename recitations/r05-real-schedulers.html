Part 1 · follow along

An MLFQ by hand

  • Q0 uses RR with q = 4, Q1 uses RR with q = 8, and Q2 uses FCFS. New jobs enter Q0.
  • Using a whole quantum demotes the job one level.
  • A job that arrives in a higher queue preempts a job that runs from a lower queue. The preempted job stays at the head of its queue and keeps the part of its quantum that it has already used.

The turnaround times are 25 for A, 5 for B, 18 for C, and 2 for D. The short jobs B and D finish from Q0, almost as if SJF had picked them. However, the scheduler never knows a burst length. The long job moves down to the bottom queue.

Part 2 · follow along

CFS vruntime

Two CPU-bound tasks share one CPU, namely T0 at nice 0 (weight 1024) and T5 at nice 5 (weight 335). Each pick runs for 4 ms. CFS runs the task with the smallest vruntime. After a task runs for Δ ms, vruntime += Δ × 1024 / weight. Therefore, T0 gains 4 per run, and T5 gains 12.23. Ties go to T0.

T0 runs for 24 ms, and T5 runs for 8 ms. Therefore, T0 gets 75% of the CPU, which matches 1024 / (1024 + 335) ≈ 0.753. Note that CFS never counts shares. It only keeps the vruntimes level, and the shares follow from the weights.

Part 3 · follow along

A lost update, on paper

counter++ consists of three machine steps, namely load, add, and store. The table below shows two threads that start from counter = 5.

stepthread 1thread 2counter
1reg = counter (5)5
2reg = counter (5)5
3reg = reg + 1 (6)5
4reg = reg + 1 (6)5
5counter = reg6
6counter = reg6

The two threads perform two increments, and one of them is lost. The three steps form a critical section, which must not be interleaved. In Lab03 Part 1, you measure how often this happens with four threads, and then you fix it with a mutex.
